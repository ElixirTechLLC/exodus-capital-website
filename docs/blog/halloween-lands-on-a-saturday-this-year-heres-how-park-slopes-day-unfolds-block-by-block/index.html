Park Slope Halloween 2026: Parade Times and Block-by-Block Guide

October 15, 2026

On Saturday, October 31, the first Park Slope street closes to cars at 10:30 a.m. and the last reopens at 10 p.m. The Park Slope Civic Council's Children's Halloween Parade, the event that gets most of the attention, steps off at 6:30 p.m. It fills the final stretch of a day that a run of separate block associations built, each one filing for its own block.

That's the main thing that changes when Halloween falls on a weekend. On a weeknight, the holiday squeezes into the hours after school and work. This year, the City's Trick-or-Streets 2026 schedule shows Park Slope streets opening to pedestrians before lunch, and the parade is the last piece of the day.

The Parade Holds Steady While Everything Around It Grows

The parade itself looks much like it has in recent years. According to the Park Slope Civic Council's 2026 announcement, marchers line up by 6 p.m. at Seventh Avenue and 14th Street. The parade steps off at 6:30, heads down Seventh Avenue, turns left on Third Street and ends at the Old Stone House and Washington Park. This year's theme is "The Haunted Forest of Brooklyn," and an orange-and-black vintage Ford leads the procession.

The route matches the one the Civic Council described in its 2023 parade post: Seventh Avenue and 14th Street, north to Third Street, left to the Old Stone House. In 2009 it looked different. A Brooklyn Paper account from that year describes a route that included a leg on Fifth Avenue.

The DOT list gives the parade a longer footprint than the march. It lists the Civic Council's event on 14th Street between Eighth and Seventh Avenues, running from 6:30 to 9 p.m. At the finish, the Brooklyn Conservatory of Music holds its annual Halloween singalong outside the Old Stone House at 7:15.

The 10:30 a.m. start comes from the block-level street closures filed around the parade.

Who Actually Closed the Streets

This is the fifth year NYC DOT has run Trick-or-Streets, turning Open Streets, plazas and other corridors into car-free space through October. Partner groups host their own car-free celebrations, and the agency coordinates the closures with the Street Activity Permit Office and NYPD. In Park Slope, nearly every partner on the 2026 Halloween list is a block-level group.

The table shows a few things. The group behind Polhemus Place appears twice: it holds Polhemus from mid-morning and a second closure on Garfield Place from 3 p.m. to 10 p.m., the latest end time in the neighborhood. Three of the blocks sit between Eighth Avenue and Prospect Park West: Montgomery Place, 11th Street and 8th Street. The two south of the parade's start, 17th Street and 21st Street, run until 9 p.m., well after the 6:30 step-off.

DOT notes on the same page that events are subject to delay or cancellation, including for weather.

How the Hours Stack

With the block permits laid out, the day breaks into three parts. The park and the institutions fill the gaps around the street closures.

Morning, park first. Prospect Park Alliance lists Creepy Crawly Halloween at the Boathouse from 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. It's free, with nature activities, experiments, a walk and treats. Green-Wood Cemetery's calendar shows a Magicians and Mediums Trolley Tour from 10 a.m. to noon on the 31st. The calendar doesn't list a price. By 11 a.m., Polhemus Place and Montgomery Place are both closed to cars.

Afternoon, the blocks fill in. 17th Street opens at noon, and the 11th Street and 8th Street block parties start at 1 p.m. From 4 to 6 p.m., the Brooklyn Conservatory of Music hosts Boo!KCM in its back garden at 58 Seventh Avenue. The free garden party has live music and candy, and the Conservatory calls it a place to sit and take a break on a trick-or-treating route. RSVP is free. Garfield Place joins at 3 p.m. and 21st Street at 3:30.

At Lakeside in Prospect Park, the Haunted Hotel's page lists special Halloween hours of 11 a.m. to 11 p.m., with timed 30-minute entry at $35 Tuesday through Sunday. The same page lists regular Saturday hours as 4 to 11 p.m., so it's best to confirm with Lakeside before planning around an early visit. Lakeside's Fall Festival attractions keep Saturday hours of 11 a.m. to 6 p.m. and offer a $20 All-Access Pass. The Alliance also lists a Haunted Carousel, with paid rides from noon to 5 p.m., October 29 through November 1.

Evening, everything heads to Third Street. Parade line-up starts at 6 p.m. at Seventh Avenue and 14th Street, the march steps off at 6:30, and the singalong at the Old Stone House begins at 7:15. The block parties on 11th Street, 8th Street and Montgomery Place wind down at 8 p.m. The rest run until 9 p.m., and Garfield Place stays closed until 10.

The Week Before Is on the Calendar Too

The weekend timing stretches things in the other direction as well. A run of smaller, named events fills the week before.

  • Sunday, October 25: The Old Stone House hosts a free Halloween Costume Showcase and Mini-Parade from 1 to 2 p.m. The same afternoon, Prospect Park Alliance holds a free community reading of The Legend of Sleepy Hollow at Lefferts Historic House from 2 to 4 p.m. RSVP is required.
  • Thursday, October 29: Sing with Suzi in the Park holds a Halloween Costume Day at JJ Byrne Playground and the Old Stone House from 10 to 11 a.m. It's free and aimed at ages 0 to 5, according to Brooklyn Bridge Parents' 2026 Halloween guide.
  • Friday, October 30: The Brooklyn Conservatory of Music screens a Halloween Movie Night: Honoring Tim Curry. Doors open at 6 p.m. and screenings start at 6:30. Tickets are $15 for adults, $10 for children under 18, and free for children 2 and under. Green-Wood's calendar lists Hereafter Hours the same night, with sessions starting at 6, 6:30 and 7 p.m.

Put the week and the day together and the parade looks smaller in context. It's one fixed point in a calendar that the Old Stone House, the Conservatory, the Alliance and a handful of block associations assembled separately.

What Isn't on Any List Yet

Two pieces of the usual Halloween picture don't show up in any 2026 source. The first is the avenues. In 2023, the Civic Council's parade post said local businesses handed out candy and treats from 3 to 6 p.m. For 2026, NYC Tourism's parade listing points visitors to trick-or-treating along Fifth and Seventh Avenues but names no participating merchants or organized event. A Fifth Avenue BID does appear on the DOT list, but that's the Bay Ridge 5th Avenue BID, holding Halloween Fest a week earlier, on October 24.

The second is the decorated houses. No 2026 list of displays turned up, and the most recent block-by-block roundup found is from 2024.

So the parts of Park Slope's Halloween you can plan around are the ones someone filed for: a block association's street permit, a Conservatory RSVP, a Civic Council line-up time. Everything else turns up on the day. That matches how Super Stoop Day played out, where the registered map captured only part of what happened on the sidewalks.
